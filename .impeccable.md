## Design Context

### Users
Ambitious, goal-oriented people — fresh graduates, career changers, and working professionals actively pursuing specific career goals. They use Path OS with purpose: to make a concrete decision or take a concrete action, not to browse passively. They are intelligent, impatient with fluff, and reward interfaces that respect their time and give them real data. Emotional state: driven, focused, slightly impatient — they want clarity fast.

Secondary audience: employers and hiring managers who are busy, pragmatic, and comparing candidates. They need density and filtering, not marketing copy.

### Brand Personality
Professional and sharp. Three words: **precise, driven, sharp**. Think LinkedIn-meets-design-studio: serious but not cold, structured but not corporate-stiff. Feels like a tool built by people who take careers seriously. No hype, no motivational filler — just clear information and actionable tools.

Anti-reference: avoid anything that looks like a generic job board or an app made in Webflow's template gallery. Gradients and multi-hue accents are part of the palette now — the failure mode to avoid is using them *inconsistently* (different gradient stops per component, arbitrary color-per-item with no rule), not using them at all.

### Aesthetic Direction
Light mode by default, with a full dark mode via toggle (not just a dimmed inverse — see Color Tokens). Off-white canvas (`oklch(0.973 0.002 236)`), cyan-tinted neutrals. **Cyan** (`oklch(0.62 0.145 196)` light / `oklch(0.78 0.145 196)` dark) is the primary brand color — logo, primary buttons, active nav state. A secondary accent family (purple, pink, amber, mint) is used for gradients and per-context highlights: purple→pink powers the "hero" summary cards (profile strength, hiring snapshot), and a cyan→purple→pink gradient marks the single highest-emphasis action tile per page (AI Coach on the candidate dashboard, Find talent on the employer dashboard).

Typography: **Bricolage Grotesque** for headings and display — geometric variable font with subtle letterform character. **Geist Sans** for body and UI text. Tabular-nums for all salary and numeric data.

One thing users will remember: the purple→pink gradient hero card at the top of both the candidate and employer dashboards — a consistent, recognizable "at a glance" summary treatment shared across both experiences.

### Design Principles
1. **Data first, decoration second** — every visual element should help the user make a decision or understand their position. Remove anything that doesn't do this.
2. **Density with breathing room** — present more information than a typical career app, but give important things space to land. Not sparse, not cluttered — editorial.
3. **Earned hierarchy, one rule per color** — not everything is a card. When a color is used to distinguish items (e.g. stat cards), it must follow one consistent rule across the whole set, not be assigned arbitrarily per item.
4. **Purposeful theme** — light by default for fast legibility in day-to-day use; dark mode is a full first-class alternative (via toggle), not an afterthought inversion.
5. **No false warmth** — no motivational micro-copy ("You're doing great!"), no emoji in interface labels. Warmth comes from good information and honest feedback, not decorative cheerfulness.

```css
/* Light (default) */
--background:  oklch(0.973 0.002 236);  --foreground: oklch(0.16 0.004 258);
--card:        oklch(0.995 0.001 236);  --border: oklch(0.90 0.004 258);
--sidebar:     oklch(0.955 0.006 270);
--brand:       oklch(0.62 0.145 196);   /* cyan — primary */
--accent-purple: oklch(0.55 0.19 300);  --accent-pink: oklch(0.62 0.20 353);
--accent-amber:  oklch(0.78 0.16 95);   --accent-amber-ink: oklch(0.50 0.14 85);
--accent-mint:   oklch(0.68 0.13 165);
--success: oklch(0.55 0.15 152);        --destructive: oklch(0.55 0.19 22);
--gradient-hero:  linear-gradient(135deg, purple, pink);
--gradient-coach: linear-gradient(120deg, cyan, purple 50%, pink 100%); /* extends hero's purple/pink backward with brand cyan */

/* Dark (toggle) — same hues, raised lightness/chroma for dark surfaces */
--background:  oklch(0.155 0.012 240);  --foreground: oklch(0.95 0.006 240);
--brand:       oklch(0.78 0.145 196);
```
Full definitions live in `app/globals.css:59-186`. Rule going forward: the purple/pink hero gradient and the cyan/purple/pink coach gradient are the only two gradient treatments in the system — don't introduce a third combination, and don't add a third flat accent color (e.g. amber fills) to plain action tiles. Per-item accent colors (e.g. stat cards) must map to one consistent rule (see Design Principle 3), not be assigned arbitrarily.

### Component Notes
- **Navigation**: Left sidebar on desktop (not top nav — signals productivity app, not marketing site). Compact icon + label, cyan indicator for active state. Background uses `bg-sidebar`, a step darker/cooler than the page background.
- **Cards**: Use sparingly. Default cards (stat tiles, list items, action tiles) are flat — a 1px border only, no shadow. Shadow is reserved for exactly two intentionally loud elements per page: the hero summary card (`.shadow-hero`) and one highlighted action tile (`.shadow-card` on a gradient fill) — "Chat with AI Coach" on the candidate dashboard, "Find talent" on the employer dashboard. Every other action tile stays flat. Don't add shadow to a plain bordered card — that's what makes the loud elements read as loud.
- **Data tables**: `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` everywhere. Role titles in primary text weight.
- **Buttons**: Primary = brand (cyan) fill. Secondary = outlined, border-strong. Ghost = text only with hover state. Never more than one primary CTA visible at once.
- **Forms (onboarding)**: Full-screen stepped layout — one decision per screen. Progress bar in amber.
- **Empty states**: Show what the user will get once filled — wireframe-style preview of the feature, not a generic "nothing here" message.
